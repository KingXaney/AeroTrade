# One look: Soft only, then Soft made compact

**Date:** 2026-11-22
**Status:** Built on two stacked branches, to be merged together: `feat/soft-only` (pull request 1,
tip `0b568ec`) and `feat/compact-soft` (pull request 2, on top of it). Not merged yet. The owner
looked at before-and-after pictures of the two together and approved compact Soft as pictured.
Supersedes in part [Themes + dashboard widgets](2026-08-24-themes-and-dashboard-widgets.md) (a
theme is no longer a palette and one of five styles) and [UX revamp](2026-10-02-ux-revamp.md) (the
labels, boxes and chrome it shaped per style are the one look's).

## Why

The owner asked for four things in one message: keep only the Soft look; control everything from
one settings page; stop everything taking so much space; and make the app a personal-finance tool
too. This spec covers the first and the third. Settings on one page and Money are later pull
requests of the same program, with specs of their own.

Mapping the code turned up one fact that shaped the work. **Soft was the roomiest of the five
styles.** It drew 17px type, a 76.5px header, an 85px rail and 102px of frame before the first
content pixel, and it forced 25.5px gaps between panels. Its rules were also outside every cascade
layer, so they beat the spacing written in components: a page that asked for `gap-3` got 25.5px
anyway. Making Soft the only look as it stood would have made every page bigger, most of all for a
new account, whose default was Minimal, the densest style.

So the work was done in two steps:

1. **Soft only.** Delete the other four styles and fold Soft into the base stylesheet, and prove
   that a reader who was already on Soft sees the same pixels.
2. **Compact Soft.** Retune Soft to be compact while it stays recognisably Soft, and move its rules
   to where a component's own spacing wins again.

The first step alone moves every default account from Minimal to the roomy 17px Soft, so the two
are merged together (risk 1 of the plan).

### What was decided

| Question | Decision | The alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Too much space" | Read as the whole app, not only Settings | Settings only |
| How Soft gets compact | Tighter everywhere: body type 17 to 16px, titles 32 to 24px, header 76 to 56px, corners 21 to 16px, gaps 25 to 16px | Tighten only the header, rail, gaps and padding; keep Soft's type size and corners |
| Palettes | All 12 stay, two of them light | One palette |
| What a new visitor sees | The dark cyan colours they had (`quiet-cyber`) in Soft's shapes, a pairing no preset ever shipped | `nord`, the colours of the Nordic Frost preset (Soft's shapes in Nord blues) |

What stays Soft, in both steps: the tinted panel fill, the four-layer shadow, the dot before each
panel heading, the pill-shaped search and tabs, the lifted current item, the brand wash behind the
page and the glow on brand buttons.

**The owner's verdict.** The owner looked at the before-and-after pairs (the landing page, sign-in,
Home, /portfolio, /trade, /settings and eleven more pages, at 1440 and 390 px, as a new visitor and
as a Soft reader) and said "I like the current look". Compact Soft as pictured is approved,
`quiet-cyber`'s bright cyan brand fill and its glow included, and new visitors keep `quiet-cyber`.
Everything changed after that fixes something broken (cut, hidden, overlapping, misaligned,
unreachable); nothing restyles what was approved.

## Pull request 1: Soft only

### A theme is a palette and Reduce motion

`Theme = {palette, reduceMotion}` (`lib/theme/resolve.ts`). The default is `quiet-cyber` with motion
on, for signed-out visitors and for accounts that never saved a theme.

- `resolveTheme` reads those two keys and no other. A `style` that an old document, cookie or open
  tab still carries is ignored, never an error.
- `isThemeInput`, what `setAppearance` accepts, checks a palette and a boolean and nothing else. A
  tab opened before this change still posts the style it had; that save lands without the style
  instead of failing with "Invalid theme".
- `database/models/user-preferences.model.ts` loses `style` from its schema and its interface.
- A preview paints the whole theme. On a hover it used to paint only the palette, because a style
  could move the page out from under the pointer. Nothing a theme holds does that now, so the
  `full` option the landing page needed is gone.
- Settings › Appearance is the twelve palette chips, Reset to default and the Reduce motion row.
  Its sentences moved to `lib/learn/copy/settings.ts` (`APPEARANCE_COPY`) with a test. The landing
  page's switcher (`components/landing/ThemeDemo`) shows six palettes, and `data-theme-demo` carries
  the palette's id. The robot's tip and the landing page count palettes only.
- The root layout's description named the news brain and the quant strategies, which only the
  owner can open, on pages anyone can open. It is now the landing page's own sentence
  (`LANDING_COPY.metaDescription`), held by that file's test.

### The cookie

The cookie is written `v2:<palette>:<0|1>`. `decodeThemeCookie` reads the whole value, in two shapes:
that one, and `v1:<palette>:<style>:<0|1>`, where the style must be one of the five ids v1 ever
wrote. The style is dropped; the palette and the motion choice are kept. Anything else is the
default theme.

The five ids are a private list in `resolve.ts` with a dated comment (written 2026-11-22). A cookie
lives a year from its last write, so a year after this ships no v1 cookie is left, and the list and
the v1 branch go.

Why read v1 at all: the root layout (`app/layout.tsx`) is the cookie's only parser, and a layout
cannot set a cookie. Read as junk, a v1 cookie would put a reader on Nord with motion reduced back
on the default for the first page they open, and for every page that never syncs the cookie with
the account: the landing and auth pages, a poker night table, the office.

### The saved theme: a save by path

A save sets `appearance.palette` and `appearance.reduceMotion` by path, never the whole
sub-document (`lib/actions/appearance.actions.ts`). A `style` stored before the one look stays where
it is. No migration removes it either. That stored field is the only record of what each reader
chose, so it is the rollback record. It is also what a build from before the one look reads while a
preview of this one shares production's database. `qa-styles` holds it in the harness's database:
the first save makes `{palette, reduceMotion}` and nothing else, and a later save changes the palette
and keeps a seeded style.

About 25 comments that explained something by a deleted style were reworded. The stylesheet went
from 132,161 to 102,694 bytes (2,690 to 2,105 lines).

### The stylesheet is Soft's

Nothing was retuned in this step. Soft's token values replaced `:root`'s, and its 28 rules lost the
`[data-style="soft"]` prefix where they stood: unlayered, in the same order, at the foot of the file.
The 17px root size became `html`'s own rule. Every rule dropped one attribute of weight, so the rules
rank among themselves as before. Against the other unlayered rules no outcome changed: this was
checked rule by rule on the source and on the compiled sheet, and a rule that now outweighs one of
the look's never styles the same box.

### Three faces, all preloaded

The look has three faces: Sora for headings (`--type-display`), Inter for the body (`--type-body`)
and JetBrains Mono for figures (`--type-mono`). The root layout still declared six and preloaded the
wrong three: Hanken Grotesk was preloaded on every page although no rule read it, and Inter, the body
face, was `preload: false`, so a first visit drew the text in the fallback and again in Inter. Now
all three are preloaded on all 40 routes: 105 kB asked for with the page, where it was 91 kB (35 kB
of it for a face nothing drew) and then 48 kB late.

`lib/theme/__tests__/fonts.test.ts` is new. Nothing ties the declarations to the stylesheet at build
time: a token that reads a variable no face writes falls back to another face without an error, and
a face no token reads is still sent to every reader. The test holds the layout, the tokens and
`app/fonts` to each other: three faces, each read by a type token, each on `<html>`, none opted out
of the preload, exactly the files they name, each family beside its licence.

### The motion guards

Two guards stop motion: the OS setting (`prefers-reduced-motion`) and the in-app toggle
(`html[data-motion="reduced"]`). Both zero the motion tokens, which makes anything timed on them a
cut. An animation timed in literal seconds is out of a token's reach, so it must be stopped by name
in both lists.

- `.animate-pulse` and `.typing-dot` joined both lists: nine call sites pulsed under Reduce motion.
  `.theme-blob` and `.animate-glow` left them, and the `[data-style] :is(...)` entry became its four
  bare classes. `StreakHero`'s pulse dropped its `motion-safe:` variant, since the guards now stop
  it under the in-app toggle too.
- `lib/theme/__tests__/motion-guards.test.ts` no longer pins a regex per brutalist rule. It reads the
  stylesheet and finds every animation timed in literal seconds or milliseconds, and requires each
  to be stopped by name in both guards. A duration counts as cut only when it is the motion token,
  or the token times plain factors: `calc(token + 1s)` is literal time. An `animate-*` utility taken
  through `@apply` is literal time too. The spinners alone keep turning, by a decision written in the
  test (`SPINNERS`). Both guards must zero both tokens, and the stylesheet must not name `data-style`.

### Same pixels for a Soft reader

The claim was that a reader already on Soft sees nothing move. It was checked three ways.

1. **Computed styles.** On 52 real pages, every computed property of every element and
   pseudo-element was equal under the old sheet and the new: in nord, paper and quiet-cyber; at
   1440, 1024 and 390 px; at rest, with `:hover`, `:focus` and `:active` forced, and under both
   reduced-motion guards.
2. **The visual sweep.** The sweep first gained an owner pass and the pages it missed (commit
   `337a061`): 55 pictures a width, signed out, as an ordinary account and as the owner. It ran on
   that commit, before any theme change, and again after, under four palettes (440 pictures each).
   Nord and Catppuccin Mocha on Soft before were compared with the same palettes after: **157 of 220
   pictures were byte-identical.** Every other difference was accounted for, each confirmed by a
   second run of the same commit:

   | What differed | Why |
   |---|---|
   | The landing page's theme demo band; Settings › Appearance, now shorter (1,487 to 900 px at 1440) | Intended: six palettes instead of six presets; no style cards |
   | /news, /topics, a topic's page, Home, the dashboard, the topic counts in Settings | Live headlines and their counts, different between any two runs |
   | The poker night lobby | A new account's avatar, rolled from its id |
   | A few pixels in a shadow band (a change of 1 or 2 in a colour channel) or on a heading dot's rim | Rasterisation; a rerun of the same commit came back identical |
   | Glyph specks in a few "before" pictures | A glitch in those pictures; the before commit's own rerun matched the after byte for byte |

3. **Again after the fonts and the review.** The fonts commit's main compiled stylesheet is
   byte-identical to the commit before it, and a Nord sweep differed only where two runs of one
   commit differ (83 of 110 pictures the same bytes). After the review fixes, a Nord sweep of all 110
   pictures against two earlier Nord sweeps: 84 identical to one of them, 23 different only where
   those two differ from each other, and 3 by rasterisation alone.

### Where the rules live

The tokens are `:root`'s. The rules sit in three places, and which one decides who wins:

| Where | What | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| `@layer base` | `html { scroll-padding-top: var(--sticky-top) }`; `text-wrap-style: balance` on headings and `pretty` on paragraphs | Defaults that anything else overrides |
| `@layer components` | The type roles (`.label-type`, `.heading-type`, `.control-type`); the row (`.row-card`, padded by the row tokens, and 44px tall when it is a link or a button under a coarse pointer); the frame (`.header`, `.header-wrapper`, `.header-bar`, `.rail`); the current item (`.lifted`); the tab track (`.tab-track`, one scrolling row on a phone); the edge fade (`[data-edge-fade]`) | A utility written at a call site still wins. In `@layer utilities` the frame came after Tailwind's own utilities and beat them; unlayered, Soft's per-element rules beat every call site |
| Unlayered | `.glass-panel`; the floating chrome (`.chrome-surface` and the shadcn `data-slot` contents); `panel-in` and the `.fx-*` motions; the look's decorations at the foot of the file (the hover lift on what can be clicked, the heading dot, the focus halo, the news tag's pill, the glow on brand buttons, the `body::before` wash); both motion guards | `.glass-panel` and the chrome must beat the radius, ring and shadow utilities beside them (invariant 7). The decorations are not density, and moving the `!important` focus halo between layers would flip which rule wins: for `!important` the layer order runs backwards |

Soft's per-element overrides (the search, the rail's tiles, the tabs, the settings tabs, the
launcher, the content wrapper, the grids' gaps, the `h1` size) are gone, and the row's padding is
the row tokens'. Each component's own classes say its size now.

**What moving rules between layers changed.** Once Soft's unlayered rules stopped beating the call
sites, things written there started to count: about 32 `RowCard` call sites with their own padding,
grids asking for `gap-2` or `gap-3`, and the six game pages' `max-w-3xl`, which narrowed them from the
full column to 768px. The tab track stopped being centred. A pixel diff cannot tell such an intended
change from a silent one, so pull request 2 is proved by computed-style checks instead: `qa-shell`
reads each density token from `:root`, turns it into pixels and compares the element with it
(Tests and suites, below). `density.test.ts` also fails when a rule reaches into a page
(`[data-app-content]`, `[data-lab-grid]`, `[data-section-tabs]`, the settings tabs) or sizes a
component that says its own size (the search, the launcher).

### The current item

The current one of a set (the rail's tile, a section's tab, a page's view tab, the settings section)
is one class, `.lifted`: a small piece of panel lifted off its track, with the panel's own fill and
layered shadow. Each component keeps its own text colour beside it. The `Tabs` primitive's current
tab used to be a filled brand pill; it is now lifted with brand text, so both kinds of tabs share one
track and one treatment.

On a near-black panel (quiet-cyber's, catppuccin's) the lifted piece was the panel's own colour on
the panel, and only its text said which item was current. The owner's pictures showed it. So the
fill has a floor: never darker than an OKLab lightness of 0.33, in the panel's own tint, ringed by a
1px hairline one step lighter that fades to nothing by a panel of lightness 0.36. A palette whose
panel is lighter than that (nord, the two light ones) draws exactly as before; a browser without
relative colours keeps the old fill. OKLab and not OKLCH, because Chrome reads a near-grey's hue as
none and painted the pill red-brown. Measured, the pill's lightness above what it sits on (rail /
section tabs / settings nav), before and after:

|---|---|---|
| quiet-cyber | 0.105 / 0.045 / 0.002 | 0.198 / 0.139 / 0.096 |
| catppuccin | 0.084 / 0.037 / 0.009 | 0.148 / 0.102 / 0.074 |
| rose-pine | 0.080 / 0.037 / 0.013 | 0.116 / 0.073 / 0.049 |

### The phone

Measured at 375x812 and 390x844 on an emulated touch phone, on ten pages:

- The header is 56px. The search is a 36x36 circle (it was a 38x47 oval); under a coarse pointer a
  transparent circle 44px across, centred on it, takes the press.
- The content's sides are 16px: the column is 358px wide at 390 (339 before) and 343px at 375.
  Nothing scrolls sideways.
- A tab track is one row that scrolls sideways below md: no wrap, its own scroller, no scrollbar
  drawn. Each tab keeps its size (`shrink-0`, `whitespace-nowrap`) and is 44px tall under a coarse
  pointer, so the track is 50px. From md the track wraps and is no scroller, which would clip the
  lifted tab's shadow.
- `hooks/useCurrentInTrack` keeps the current tab in sight. It sets the track's own `scrollLeft` and
  never calls `scrollIntoView`, which would scroll the window too and pull a page opened at an anchor
  back up. It places the tab at once on load and glides there on a change, unless motion is reduced.
  The sums are pure and unit-tested (`lib/shell/track-scroll`): 1.5rem of the row (24px at the
  default size) stays in view beside the current tab, a tab that fits with little room is centred,
  and one wider than the track lines up at its start.
- The hook also names the ends of the row with more to scroll to (`data-edge-fade`), where the row
  fades out over 1.5rem (`EDGE_FADE_REM`), the same room it keeps beside the current tab, so the
  current tab is never under the fade. A row cut at the screen's edge reads as a row that goes on, not
  as a clipped word. Only a box that scrolls sideways is marked.
- A row that is a link or a button is at least 44px tall under a coarse pointer.
- The launcher is 48px below md (56px from md), and Home's Expand sits under the terrain's heading on
  a phone, out of the corner the launcher floats in.
- The sheets' and dialogs' slides joined both motion guards, by their `data-slot`.

### The motion vocabulary

The look's motions are small, and each is a class of its own name moving a real element. No
pseudo-element animates, since a guard naming the class would not reach it. Each is unlayered,
beside `panel-in`, and stopped by name in both motion guards. Each rests in its own static style,
which is all a guard's `animation: none` leaves. `motion-guards.test.ts` finds every
`@keyframes fx-*` in the stylesheet and requires a class of the same name, outside any layer, in both
guards, and used by some file in `app/` or `components/`.

A class lands in the pull request of its first user, so no rule ships unused:

| Class | What moves | First user | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.fx-beacon` | One halo breathes round "now": a glow in the element's own colour grows out of it and fades, then a pause. 2.4s, a loop | The market-open dot (`components/stocks/MarketStatus`, its class from `lib/stocks/market-dot.marketDotClass`, on Home, /trade and /watchlist) | Landed (PR 2). Money's today tick follows (PR 5) |
| `.fx-shimmer` | A soft band sweeps over a placeholder, again and again. 1.6s | `components/primitives/Skeleton`, each bar (the box carries `data-skeleton`) | Landed (PR 2) |
| `.fx-sheen` | One band of light crosses a headline panel once as it arrives, after the panel's own rise, on the motion token | `Panel`'s `sheen`: Home's accounts panel and /portfolio's account summary, one a view | Landed (PR 2). Money's month panel follows (PR 5) |
| `.fx-rise` | A bar or spoke grows from its base | Money's month clock, six-month strip and plan meter | Waits for PR 5 |
| `.fx-in` | A new row settles (4px and opacity), fresh rows only | A new Money entry (PR 5); import rows (PR 6) | Waits for PR 5 |
| `.fx-draw` | A stroke draws to its resting length | Money's river | Waits for PR 7 |
| `.fx-wipe` | A wrapper's clip-path opens left to right | The net worth chart | Waits for PR 8 |

The beacon's halo animates plain `currentColor`, because Chrome animates a `color-mix()` over
`currentColor` in one jump instead of a fade. The shimmer's band is the middle half of an image twice
the bar's width, so at either end of the sweep, and at rest, it lies wholly past an edge. The sheen
is an empty span behind the panel's content (`z-index: -1` in the panel's isolated stack); its
margin and border are set to 0 so a panel's `space-y` or `divide-y` cannot reach it. Measured: the
sheen crosses once (1.2s after a 0.2s delay) and rests at 110% of the panel's width, off the surface;
under either guard it is already at rest and the beacon has no halo.

### Home: accounts first, the terrain as a strip

Home's order is now the reader's own figures first: the title and its chips; the paper accounts
(`#home-accounts`, the total `[data-home-total]`, the page's sheen) beside the next step; the momentum

Money's strip will go above the accounts (PR 5); there is no placeholder for it now.

The terrain is the landing page's `MomentumTerrain` at a new `size="strip"`:

  Its one "What these mean" sits under both.
- It is frameless, like the landing hero, and inset as a panel's content is (a default panel's

## Known limits and what is left open

- **A fragment opened on a fresh load lands up to 6px high**, 65.5 to 67px instead of 72 (still clear
  of the 56px bar): the browser aims while `panel-in`'s 6px rise is under way. A hash change after
  load lands at exactly 72. This predates the work; fixing it would change the approved arrival
  motion, so it is the owner's call.
- **The beacon has never been seen on a server-rendered page while the market was open**: the harness
  cannot set the session. Its class is unit-tested both ways. Look once during market hours on Home,
  /trade or /watchlist.
- **No sheet, dialog, menu, popover or select animates at all.** Their classes say `data-open:` and
  `data-closed:`, which compile to `[data-open]`, but Radix writes `data-state`. So the sheet and
  dialog entries in the guards stop nothing yet, and menus, popovers and selects are in no guard: they
  would animate under Reduce motion once the variant is fixed.
- **Two loading motions in one panel**: the office's and the poker table's loading panels pulse an
  outline above shimmering Skeleton lines. The 3D scenes' veils, the invite QR placeholder and Home's
  terrain veil still pulse (`animate-pulse`, in both guards).
- **Home's terrain draws its controls and reading at hydration**, pushing what is under it down by
  about 80px at 1440 (WebGL cannot be known on the server). On touch the strip's tooltip shows only
  while the finger is down.
- The strip's surface covers about half of the strip at rest at 1440 (up to 65% while turning).
  Filling more would let the turning surface clip.
- Before hydration a phone's tab row is shown unscrolled and without its fade.
- On a phone a scrolling tab track clips the lifted tab's shadow; light palettes were not looked at.
- View tabs inside a panel (a stock's reference tabs) on dracula, gruvbox, one-dark, monokai,
  solarized and nord read by their shadow and brand text: the current pill is within 0.013 lightness
  of its track, as nord's always was.
- On Portfolio's first screen at 390 and 375 the launcher covers the end of two lines of text, though
  no link or button.
- Older than this work, and left as the owner approved them: /trade's market scan draws its views as
  solid cyan tabs (a follow-up could draw them with the `Tabs` primitive, keeping the label "Market
  views"); `StreakHero`'s heading is 24px, the page title's size (a follow-up could make it
  `text-xl`, as `StreakMini`'s is); short dashboard widgets leave space under them; the daily puzzle
  pages keep about 60px between the hint line and the buttons; the phone's menu button (`MobileNav`)
  is a 36px target, which could take the search's 44px ring.
- Design calls left open: a game page's "← All games" link is inline, so nothing spaces it from the
  title (3px tighter than before, with the title's 30px line); on a phone the watchlist's cells sit
  30px in from the panel's edge (the panel's 16px and the row's 14px), roomier sideways than other
  lists.
- The lab's progress card is stretched to the review card beside it: 118px for 87px of content, as
  approved.
- The landing hero's 500px comes from `MomentumTerrain`'s own size map; no token holds it.
- No suite holds the radii (16/12/12px) or the launcher's size (48 and 56px), and none holds the
  current item's floor on quiet-cyber: comparing a relative colour by computed style is not reliable,
  and a pixel check is not cheap.
- Without WebGL the strip's tooltip check becomes a NOTE, since the flat heatmap has no hover.
- Two older races in the suites showed once each and passed when run again: `qa-trading`'s "the desk
  does not move when the tape's and the chart's frames arrive" can read a streamed page that is still
  hidden; `qa-poker-night-modes` can miss the "Next hand" countdown on a polling page.
