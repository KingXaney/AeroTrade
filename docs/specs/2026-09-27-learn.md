# Learn: the app teaches from its own data

**Status:** Wave 1 merged to main in 37400a4 (PR #24); Wave 2 is PR #27; Wave 3 implemented on
`feat/learn-wave-3`, stacked on Wave 2 (not merged). Every slice the plan scheduled is built; see
"What comes next" for what it deferred.

## Why

The owner asked for the site to teach "finances and the stock market and everything in between".
A ten-agent design panel scored fifty-five ideas and found that the app already held the teaching
material and was not showing it: `StrategyRun` stores each morning's full signal board with every
row's verdict and every order's reason (the file calls itself "the educational record"),
`PaperTrade.reason` existed but user orders never filled it, the catalog's `beginnerLine` was
authored and tested for all eight strategies and rendered nowhere, `SignalColumn.help` was wired to
the column tooltip and set by no column, and the chat had fifteen tools, no rate limit, and a chip
that said "Should I add NVDA?".

So the product teaches from live data, with no model call except the chat, and it never turns a
description into a recommendation. The owner chose all four directions the panel pitched, plus an
AI tutor behind a rate limit and an audit of the surfaces that dispensed tips, and declined a
learner-model switch: nothing asks a user how experienced they are, and nothing from sign-up is
persisted for it.

## The rules (AGENTS.md invariant 12)

1. **One banned-word module.** `lib/learn/banned.ts` is the list of words that turn a description
   into advice, in two tiers: `advice` (verdicts and instructions about money, banned in every
   voice, prompts included) and `copy` (adds a bare "should", "consider", better/best/beat, an
   imperative buy/sell — for learner-facing text). A clause that begins "Never", "No", "Don't" is a
   prohibition, not advice, and is stripped before matching. Every learner-facing sentence is a pure
   export under `lib/learn/copy/<feature>.ts`, and every feature's test calls `findBanned` over it.
   The catalog's column help and beginner lines, the chat chips, the tool descriptions and the model
   prompts are held to it too.
2. **One glossary.** `lib/learn/glossary.ts` defines every metric the app computes (formulas cited
   from the code that computes them), the news concepts the starter topics match (keyed exactly to
   the starter keywords, so a matched article term is a glossary key), and the Navigator's rails.
   `resolveTerm(query)` and `searchGlossary(query)` never build a RegExp from the query. A signal
   column names its entry (`SignalColumn.glossary`) and shows that entry's short text as its help.
3. **One placement rule for "what does this mean".** A label carries `title=` from the glossary via
   `<Term>`; each panel has at most one collapsed `<WhatTheseMean>` disclosure listing only the terms
   that panel shows (the touch-reachable twin of every title); every automated fill has exactly one
   "What the rule saw" disclosure (on its trade-log row; a latest-decision order carries one only
   when it did not fill); the chat's "Ask in chat" link lives only inside those two
   disclosures, never as a per-row icon, and the dashboard widgets that reuse those components never
   pass the opt-in prop. `beginnerLine` renders in the strategy detail header, at wide span in the
   quant-strategies widget, and once per strategy on the `/learn` index — nowhere else.
4. **Copy is measurement, never instruction.** Returns side by side with no comparative verb; the
   Navigator is described by mechanism, never as working or failing; amber only for something
   actually wrong; numbers above the fold, prose in `<details>`, caveats once per panel.
5. **Derived state, not a learner flag.** Surfaces self-limit from rows (trade count, account age,
   windows) plus stamp-once fields in a no-default `learn` sub-schema on `UserPreferences`.
6. **Bounded reads only.** No board-carrying aggregate on a request path (`getLatestRuns` belongs to
   a detail page); replay reads project only the traded symbols' rows; every bar read is bounded.

### Wave 2 deviations from the plan

- **Decoder.** A clause's `rail` names the `lib/strategies/config.ts` constant (`CASH_FLOOR` is
  1%), not the glossary's `cash-floor`, which is the Navigator's 10%. Each template has an owner
  strategy whose catalog parameters apply unless the caller passes the owner's own definition.
  The grammar also decodes data issues and the engine's stale-day skip; `LatestDecision` still
  prints skipped orders and data issues raw. A test fails on any template nothing emits, so 3.5's
  navigator grammar must land with `scoreUniverse`/`diffToOrders` fixtures.
- **Parameters.** Narrators and the decoder read `def.params` through `findParam` (never throws),
  and slot counts come from `def.slots`, which is what `decideRanked` uses. The golden-cross
  narrator names its averages from `fast`/`slow` but reads the `sma50`/`sma200` value keys the rule
  always writes.
- **`/history`.** The decoder is placed on strategy pages only (placement rule 3); the QA check on
  `/history` is negative: the reason shows, with no decoder and no Ask link.
- **Portfolio.** The risk caption says "one holding", not "one company" (a holding can be an ETF),
  and reads its 20% and 10% from `lib/navigator/config.ts`.
- **Ledger.** A note's epoch is the ledger's. Every per-account trade read — the cached
  `getTradeLedger`, `getTradeHistory`, the CSV export, the comparison table and the chat's sell
  totals — starts at the account's `inceptionAt` (`epochTrades`), so a reset that crashed before
  deleting the old epoch's trades leaves nothing a page counts. `getTradeHistory` is a bounded read
  (the newest N) for callers without the ledger; `/portfolio` and `/trade` slice the ledger, which
  throws on a failed read so pages hide what they derive from it instead of showing "0 trades".
  Indexes: `{accountId, createdAt, _id}` for the ledger's sort, `{userId, source, side, createdAt}`
  for the first user sell. The CSV quotes every string cell (a `;` locale must not split a note) and
  neutralises formulas.
- **Income receipts.** The printed APY is cash-weighted, because a plain mean of daily APYs misses
  by dollars once cash and rate both move. The new dividend index keys on `dividend` too.
- **Tutor.** Learner figures exist for the plan's six keys only; `decodeReason` runs without a
  definition. A new chat tool also needs a name in the prompt's Capabilities list (a test holds it).
- **Today's lesson.** Daily strategies get no rebalance moment (they trade every day). The
  First-week checklist loads only its onboarding facts (`onboardingFacts`, a `cache()` hit on the
  page's own availability read); `learnFacts` feeds Today's lesson alone, whose concept aggregate is
  a lazy key read only when no moment wins. A term a lesson defines is the glossary's own line
  ("Win rate: …"). An exact 5.00% drop counts (a 1e-9 epsilon).
- **Ticket APY.** `getCashApy` reads the latest stored ^IRX, `cache()`d so a render reads the rate
  once, and goes blank once it is older than the income job's staleness rule (`usableRate`, shared
  with the watermark). The clause stays off the compact dashboard ticket, whose line already drops
  the cash left it describes, and the ticket's definitions list APY only while the buy line
  carries the clause (`ticketTerms`).
- **Process.** 2.2, 2.3a and 2.3b resumed uncommitted work from interrupted runs; their tests
  were reviewed and mutation-checked rather than written test-first.

### Wave 3 deviations from the plan

- **What-if lab: a nightly grid for every strategy, no live action.** The plan asked for a
  timing spike on a dev route, locally and on a Vercel preview, deciding per strategy between a
  live, rate-limited action (p95 load + simulate at most 4 s) and a nightly grid. A preview
  deployment would have read the production database from a preview build, so the spike ran
  locally only (an M5 on a synthetic 1,017-bar universe, the script kept outside the repository):
  one `simulateStrategy` has a median of 20–458 ms and a p95 of at most 497 ms per strategy; the
  whole grid takes ≈ 7.5 s (≈ 9 s at 1,260 bars), the large-cap strategies ≈ 1.6–2.2 s each, and a
  serverless function is likely two to three times slower — past the bar for the large-cap rules
  once the bar load is added. So there is no spike route, no Build A, no `WHATIF_MODE` and no
  what-if rate limit: every strategy gets the precomputed grid, which is therefore persisted (the
  plan's "nothing persisted" was Build A's). At most four single-knob variants per strategy
  (buy-and-hold has none), stored as an array beside `variantsVersion` (variant ids contain dots,
  so never Mongo keys), recomputed only when the backtest was rebuilt or the grid moved, one
  Inngest step per strategy whose failure is logged only; "resimulate" forces a recompute. Only
  positions with a stored variant (plus the catalog) are offered, and a variant's trades are not
  shown (their reasons would print the catalog's labels). The labels live in
  `lib/learn/copy/whatif.ts`, not in `whatif.ts`.
- **Daily quiz.** `getRecentRuns(strategyIds, since)` takes the window floor explicitly (one
  bounded point read per strategy). "Why" questions offer four options, one per verdict, falling
  back to `STATE_MEANING`; a reading that names the asked symbol is never an option.
- **Time in the market.** The default start (the learner's first inception) is clamped like a
  typed date, to [today − 1,560 days, today − 91 days]. Monthly deposits are held as units of the
  total-return index (dividends reinvested, like the benchmark). "Underwater" counts sessions below
  the dollars deposited so far. The read loads from the earlier of the start and today − 24 months,
  so the start-date table always has its rows.
- **Luck or skill.** The window ends on the latest SPY session or the last snapshot's date; SPY is
  held like the random portfolios (whole shares, dividends as cash); the cache is a module-level
  memo of at most 64 windows for the ET day; "had you held" covers the five names sold most
  recently.
- **Brain.** `lib/navigator` changed only by exports (`HOLDING_REASON`, `TOP_QUINTILE_FRACTION`),
  to stay clear of PR #26, whose neutral-news reason is decoded literally (tested with the
  literal). "What the Navigator saw" has no "Ask in chat" link, because `explainTerm` decodes
  strategy reasons only. The leaderboard's line reads "its slow weight reached 5".
- **Stock page.** `getCompanyProfile` gained the keyless guard as well as `getFinancials`; the
  dividend yield is `currentDividendYieldTTM` only; a P/E at or below zero prints "none" with the
  reason; values every watching rule shares (the run date, the close) are stated once (invariant
  8). Its verdict badges carry `data-rules-see-verdict`, not the board's `data-verdict`.
- **Digest.** `buildLessonSectionHtml` takes the app's base URL rather than the Learn URL: a moment
  links to its own page (`/portfolio`, `/portfolio#income`, `/strategies/<id>`), a concept to
  `/learn#<key>`. The allow-list is `lessonSectionLinks`, exactly the links the section builds (a
  non-http headline prints as text and is not listed). The heading is `LESSON_COPY.emailHeading`;
  every other sentence is the widget's copy or the glossary's own line. Nothing is stamped from the
  email — "Got it" stays on the widget, and a moment already marked is not mailed — and a moment
  the noon run did not see as yesterday's (a dividend credited late) is not mailed afterwards.
- **Carried as-is.** `SimulatedStats` moved out of the strategy page in 3.3b with its inline
  hints, which are not yet in `lib/learn/copy`.

## Decisions taken for the owner (override any of them)

Only `getting-started` joins `DEFAULT_LAYOUT`; the lesson and quiz widgets (Waves 2–3) are
library-only. The first-trade mission deep-links `/trade?symbol=SPY` and calls SPY "the fund every
strategy is measured against". Mission 5 is Navigator enrolment described by mechanism only. The
chat ceiling defaults to 200 requests a day for everyone and is tunable by environment. The welcome
email keeps the user's own chosen sector but no stock adjectives. The tutor never reads a user's
"why" notes (Wave 2). The what-if lab is a nightly precomputed grid for every strategy, never a
live run (Wave 3, above). The digest's lesson has no switch of its own: it rides under the email
opt-out, like the market summary it sits under.

## The 3-minute demo path

`/strategies/rsi2-mean-reversion`: hover RSI(2) and SMA200, open "Read this board", open "Try it —
